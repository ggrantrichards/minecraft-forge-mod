# The Astral End - the trailer, cut as an anime opening. The game (client/ClientTrailer.java) films the
# shots, in the order of their lines; tools/trailer/opening.py cuts them together as the "clip" lines
# say, on the beat of the music (a beat every 12 frames), and explains them. A shot:
#
#   <shot> <frames> id=<name> [key=value ...] |
#
# <shot> is a viewpoint the worldgen survey chose (astralend-shots.txt). The camera starts there and
# moves over <frames> frames (24 a second):
#   fwd, side, up     blocks to travel forward (along the starting view), to the right, and up
#   yaw               degrees to pan (positive: to the right); pitch=a:b tilts from a to b
#   orbit             degrees to circle the focus (positive: counter-clockwise from above)
#   focus             what to orbit and look at: the shot's mob (default), or "hit" (where the view
#                     first meets the ground)
#   track             blocks per frame that the camera and the mob glide along together
#   roll              degrees of camera roll (roll=a:b from a to b), eased over the shot or between
#                     frames rollFrom and rollTo
#   ease              both (default), in, out or none
#   ai                the shot's mob moves about as it likes (otherwise it holds still)
#   cam=third         a third-person camera that follows the player (or a fight) from dist blocks off
#                     and height up, starting angle degrees round, circling orbit degrees over the
#                     shot, pulling back pull and rising rise; follow is how closely it keeps up
#   fight=<beat>      a beat of the Curator fight, played in survival in the reliquary (see
#                     client/TrailerFight.java); cont carries on from the beat before
# and things to happen: wake@N, bridges@N (on frame N), flare=<phase>, clear.
# Before filming, the camera flies each shot's whole path once and waits until everything it will see
# has loaded and been drawn.
# A creature summoned for a shot that walks stands on the ground under it.
